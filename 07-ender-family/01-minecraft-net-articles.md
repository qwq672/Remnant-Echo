### Ender Dragon
- **来源类型**：minecraft.net
- **原文链接**：<https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/ender-dragon>
- **发布时间**：2025-08-21T15:00:00.000+0000
- **页面摘要**：Dragons were talked about as being part of the Minecraft universe as early as 2010, but it wasn’t until version 1.0 of the game – the Adventure Update, released in November 2011 – that the Ender dragon was added. Here's what we know about it...

**原文引用：**

> Some people like to play games, while others like to complete them. The first group will spend ages exploring and seeing everything a virtual world has to offer, while the latter will compete to see how quickly they can claim victory. If you fall into the second group, then today’s Mob of the Month is dedicated to you – we’re profiling Minecraft’s final boss – the Ender Dragon.

> Dragons were talked about as being part of the Minecraft universe as early as 2010, but it wasn’t until version 1.0 of the game – the Adventure Update, released in November 2011 – that the Ender Dragon was added. Other features added at the same time included enchanting, brewing, and breeding animals.

> The Ender Dragon is a huge, hostile monster that attacks you as soon as you set foot into the End. It’s black and grey, with glowing purple eyes just like an Enderman, and it flies around assaulting players with fireballs and breath attacks.

> To handle this beast, you’re gonna need some specialist equipment. A sword, ideally enchanted, is a must – you’ll also need a bow or crossbow with plenty of ammunition so you can hit it while flying. Wear your strongest armour, and bring plenty of healing items – golden apples or healing potions. You’ll also likely want to bring some empty glass bottles to collect dragon’s breath.

> The fight is tricky, but very much possible. Run to the centre of the island and try to stay within the circle of towers to avoid falling off into the void. You can start shooting arrows at the dragon if you want, but you’ll quickly see that it heals up damage through the crystals on top of the towers. Destroy the crystals with your ranged weapons as fast as you can (ideally while they’re healing the dragon because it’ll cause a burst of damage if you do).

> Once the crystals are destroyed, it’s finally time to slay the dragon itself. While flying you can shoot it with a bow, but sometimes it’ll fly down and hover over the portal – and here it’s immune to arrow damage. Instead, rush in with your sword and try to get in as many hits as you can.

> Eventually after doing enough damage, the beast will be slain. You’ll get a tonne of XP orbs, a dragon egg, the End Gateway portal which allows you to access the End’s outer islands, and a portal back to the Overworld. When you jump into the exit portal, you’ll get to read the End Poem, written by Irish poet Julian Gough, and the credits - which mark the official “End” of the game. Completionists, your work here is done.

> But this is Minecraft, so of course you can play on – if you want to. There’s lots more to explore in the End, the Nether, and the Overworld, and you can even respawn the Ender Dragon if you want to. You do this by surrounding the exit portal with End Crystals. It’s fun to finish the game, but the world you’ve created will live on, and on, and on.



### Ender Pearl
- **来源类型**：minecraft.net
- **原文链接**：<https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/ender-pearl>
- **发布时间**：2025-02-18T16:00:00.000+0000
- **页面摘要**：Ender pearls were added to the game in September 2011 as part of the Adventure Update, but initially they didn’t do anything. It took until version 1.0.0 of the game, following a suggestion from Reddit user isJesus, before they got their current functionality.

**原文引用：**

> Teleportation is one of those things that’s totally routine and unremarkable in videogames, but completely, physically impossible in the real world. Imagine being able to jump from point A to point B without passing through the space in between! I think we should all take a moment to pause and appreciate what a joy and a pain it would be if our item of the month – the Ender pearl – existed IRL.

> I raise this because the Ender pearl is Minecraft’s answer to “how do you teleport?” They were added to the game in September 2011 as part of the Adventure Update, but initially they didn’t do anything. It took until version 1.0.0 of the game, following a suggestion from Reddit user isJesus, before they got their current functionality.

> Ender pearls aren’t super easy to get hold of. The easiest way is probably to defeat an Enderman. There’s a 50:50 chance it’ll drop one. A Looting enchantment on your weapon will increase the number you can get to a maximum of 4. They can also be found in strongholds and trial chambers, in some woodland mansions, and can be traded with cleric villagers and piglins.

> To use an Ender pearl, just hold it in your hand and press the use button. But wait! Before you do that, you’ll want to be careful about which direction you’re facing. Using the Ender pearl involves throwing it in front of you, and you’ll teleport to where it lands. If that’s a pool of lava then... Well, don’t say I didn’t warn you.

> The other thing you’ll want to do with Ender pearls is to craft them into Eyes of Ender, which allow you to locate a stronghold and access the End dimension. To do this, pop it in a crafting grid with some blaze powder. But my advice is to only convert one at a time, as you need them, because there’s no way of converting them back again afterwards.

> As noted above, teleportation isn’t a thing in the real world (damn you, physics). But pearls are! These beautiful and valuable gemstones are not dug out of mines like most other gems, but are instead dug out of the bodies of living creatures.

> Technically speaking, a pearl is created by shellfish in the same way that human bodies form scars. When one of these creatures’ shells is harmed in some way, its body releases a substance called “nacre” as part of the healing process. This builds up in layers inside the shellfish until it forms a spherical object.

> In nature, pearls form by chance and they’re incredibly rare – hundreds of oysters or mussels would need to be opened (and killed in the process) before locating even one. Luckily, we’ve developed a way of farming pearls without killing so many creatures – a tiny bit of shell from another shellfish is inserted into the host creature’s shell, which reliably forms a pearl over time. Today, about 95% of pearls on the market are made in this way.



### Ender Chest
- **来源类型**：minecraft.net
- **原文链接**：<https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/ender-chest>
- **发布时间**：2025-06-05T19:28:00.000+0000
- **页面摘要**：Ender Chests are really, really handy. Put some stuff into one, and you can then access that stuff from any other Ender Chest in any dimension – including an Ender Chest that you’ve brought with you and placed down solely for that purpose.

**原文引用：**

> Ender chests are really, really handy. Put some stuff into one, and you can then access that stuff from any other Ender chest in any dimension – including an Ender chest that you’ve brought with you and placed down solely for that purpose. Effectively, this means your lesser-used tools (e.g. hoe, flint and steel, shears) and building materials, or your most valuable finds, can sit in a single inventory slot so you can carry home more cobblestone from your mining trips.

> Ender chests were added to Minecraft in August 2012, alongside emerald ore, golden apples and wooden slabs. They were based on a design suggested by Redditor soapawake about a year beforehand. Soapawake’s original recipe called for an ender pearl surrounded by wooden planks, but user @fivedollarlunch suggested in the comments it be surrounded by obsidian instead. Jeb then tweaked it to require an eye of ender – and that gave us the recipe we have today.

> As well as their dislocated storage potential, Ender chests have a few other interesting qualities. The contents of an Ender chest are unique to each player, meaning that they’re a great way to secure valuable items on a multiplayer server – if a thief opens one up, all they’ll see is their own Ender chest inventory.

> Ender chests also emit a small amount of light, but on the flipside they can’t be made into a double chest, placed onto donkeys, mules or llamas, or pushed with pistons. Be wary, too, of opening one up near a piglin – they’ll want to know what’s inside, and they can be very forceful when they want something.

> You might not think that there’s any real-world equivalent of an Ender chest, but you’d be wrong. Once upon a time, if you wanted to hear a piece of music, then you’d need to be in the same place as the musician and they’d need to want to play it.

> Then along came recording technology. People figured out that you could capture the vibrations in the air produced by a sound and capture them on paper, wax, or soft metal. Later, they figured out that they could copy these recordings, and reprint them onto vinyl and then magnetic tape. Before long, a musician could perform once and could then be heard millions of times, all over the world.

> But then something else happened. People figured out that you could digitise those recordings – turning them into long strings of 1s and 0s, like a redstone pulse. Today, you don’t need a vinyl record, or a cassette tape, to listen to music – the Ender chests of the real world are data centers where you can store more music, or photos, or movies, or documents, or Minecraft worlds, than any one person could ever experience in a lifetime.

> In fact, it’s thanks to a datacentre that you’re reading these very words – and I think we can both agree that it’s very much preferable to me coming round to your house and shouting in your ear about how great data centers are. So profound thanks go to both datacentres and to Ender chests, for saving us all a lot of bother.



### End city
- **来源类型**：minecraft.net
- **原文链接**：<https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/end-city>
- **发布时间**：2025-07-18T14:18:00.000+0000
- **页面摘要**：There are a plethora of things to do in the End. Excitingly, some of the most exotic goodies can be found really quite close to the site of your grand victory over the Ender dragon. Today we're helping you hunt down the End City.

**原文引用：**

> Once you’ve slain the Ender dragon and rolled credits, Minecraft is done right? Wrong. There are a plethora of other pastimes to pursue and lots more loot to locate. Excitingly, some of the most exotic goodies can be found really quite close to the site of your grand victory over the Ender dragon. Today I’m gonna help you hunt down our structure of the month – the End city.

> First though, a little history lesson. End Cities were first unveiled to players in June 2015 just before Minecon 2015, and then released in the Combat Update in February 2016. Fun fact – right before they were designed, Jens 'Jeb' Bergensten was visiting China, and so it’s very plausible that their unique look was influenced by the design of traditional Asian pagodas.

> Now, let’s get down to business. Finding an End city isn’t too hard but it does need a little work. First, you’ll want to get yourself a lot of Ender pearls, which should be easy enough once you’ve defeated the Ender dragon, as you’ll be standing on a big platform with a lot of Endermen on it.

> You’ll also want to bring a bunch of blank locator maps and an Ender chest to make sure you don’t lose things if you die. Good weaponry and armor, food and potions, a carved pumpkin to avoid angry endermen, and some blocks to build with are handy too. Oh – and a bucket of water. A bucket of water is a great way to avoid dying to falls into the void – position the flow so it’s close to where you’re exploring, and if you drop then try to fly into it. You’ll then be able to swim back up unharmed.

> Ready? Good. Hurl an Ender pearl into the End gateway and you’ll be instantly teleported to the End’s outer islands. Hopefully you’ll be able to see an End city from where you land, but if not then jump between islands until you do – either by building a bridge or hurling an Ender pearl. Don’t worry – if the pearl falls into the void then you won’t be teleported. Eventually you’ll see a structure that looks like a multistorey branched tower, ideally with a ship parked at it near the top. Prepare yourself for danger, and head on in.

> You’re unlikely to get lost in an End city, but death from falling is a real risk because the shulkers that inhabit them will often apply the levitation effect. Feather Falling enchantments can help, but really you’re just gonna need to be careful – try to stay under low ceilings when you can, and if you do find yourself rising unexpectedly then either drink a bucket of milk to void the effect or place water below you just before you hit the ground to void the damage.

> Look out for treasure rooms in the End cities (and stash the goodies in your Ender chest so you don’t lose them), but really the reason you’re here is to collect a couple of items that don’t appear anywhere else – shulker shells to make shulker boxes, and an elytra. The latter can only be found on the ships I mentioned previously – bridge or Ender pearl your way over to its deck, kill the shulkers living within, grab the dragon head if you want to, and then head downstairs to pick up the elytra. Once you’ve found it, don’t forget to use it to descend safely to the ground again.

> You can sustain yourself for quite a long time in the End by eating chorus fruit (stay away from ledges when you do), but eventually you’ll need to return home – which can be done by either returning to the portal you arrived in, or by putting your most important gear in your Ender chest (don’t forget your armor) and hurling yourself into the void. I prefer the former, but hey – no judgment if the latter sounds easier!



### Meet the Enderlings
- **来源类型**：minecraft.net
- **原文链接**：<https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/meet-enderlings>
- **发布时间**：2021-07-25T14:45:02.465+0000
- **页面摘要**：Endermen endier than before!

**原文引用：**

> We are only three days away from the release of Echoing Void, the latest Minecraft Dungeons DLC. That's literally around the corner! Considering the otherworldly threats that await you, I would step away from the corner this instant and instead direct all your attention elsewhere. Like this story, which happens to have juicy details to share about said threats! This is why you look straight ahead (and don't dig straight down)!

> The only known mobs in The End have so far been endermen and shulkers. However, with the launch of Echoing Void, we can now add a new threat to the list: the enderlings. These malicious mobs come in three different subcategories: watchlings, blastlings, and snarelings. Although they may all be smaller than their endermen cousins, they still pack a powerful punch with unique abilities of their own. A fun challenge for players, but also for the developerlings during their creation.

> The most important thing for our artists to nail was giving each of the enderlings a distinct look, emphasising their core gameplay abilities and ensuring they maintained the otherworldly feel the enderman is known for. The watchling is most like the enderman of the three. With its many eyes, it’s always on the lookout for intruders. When it spots a player, it instantly teleports right beside them and lash out with a vicious melee attack.

> The blastling is the ranged combat specialist of the enderling family, it rapidly fires gooey projectiles from its bulky arms at the nearest player. The speed and volume at which it fires projectiles mean that players have a brand-new type of ranged threat to deal with in the End.

> Lastly, the snareling is by far the weirdest member of the family (and the dev team's favourite), and is very similar to a spider in its behavior. When it spots a player, it awkwardly moves into range before rearing back on its tripod like arms and spitting a giant gooey splat at them. Any player unlucky enough to be hit by this attack will find themselves stuck to the spot, which isn’t even the worst part. What happens next is that all snarelings in the area teleport to the trapped hero and viciously start attacking them with giant slaps from their windmill-like limbs!

> The team at Double Eleven had a lot of fun in designing this new line-up of mobs for Echoing Void – but it wasn’t all smooth sailing, according to Matt.

> A common trait for all enderlings is teleportation. If threatened, they can teleport to safety. During development, we had watchlings teleporting away to a hole in the terrain we didn’t even know was there, taunting players from this safe spot. We also had blastlings teleporting up onto a nearby wall and raining down shots on players below – with no chance of them returning fire. Enderlings have a lot of personality and love causing problems, it seems.

> Youngsters these days. Back in my day, endermen used to have style, stealing your dirt blocks before teleporting away with an ominous chuckle (that sound is a chuckle, right?). If you want to cause some ruckus and be a troublemaker yourself, know that The End awaits you in Echoing Void on July 28.



### Meet the Shulker
- **来源类型**：minecraft.net
- **原文链接**：<https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/meet-shulker>
- **发布时间**：2017-01-30T14:01:21.000+0000
- **页面摘要**：Get under the shell of the camouflaged critter

**原文引用：**

> Adventurers should explore the outer islands of the End with care - in among the blocks of purpur hang these cannily camouflaged critters. Get too close, and their block-shaped shells pop open, allowing the mollusc-like Shulker to launch a homing projectile!

> It all started when we added the End City. 'End City' was a work-in-progress name, actually, but it just kind of stuck. We wanted to make a new mob for the area and, initially, I was working on a new type of golem you could craft, but I couldn’t come up with a fun design.

> Then I had the idea of a mob which lived inside the voxel grid. So, I made this cube-shaped mob that’d open up and shoot projectiles that follow the grid, chasing after the player by flying in straight paths and then doing 90 degree turns.

> When you get hit by one of the missiles, you start to levitate. The reasoning was that you might use the levitation to get across from island to island, allowing yourself to get hit on purpose. But it’s a risk-reward scenario. It’s likely that you will float up and be hit more times and then fall to your death. It’s a big incentive to seek out the Elytra first as you tackle the End - then you can just glide down to safety, assuming you manage to get out of the Shulker’s range!
