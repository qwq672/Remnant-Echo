### Meet the Villagers in Minecraft
- **来源类型**：minecraft.net
- **原文链接**：<https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/meet-villagers>
- **发布时间**：2017-03-12T18:30:00.000+0000
- **页面摘要**：Did you know that you can trade with the villagers in Minecraft? Learn about the different types of Minecraft villagers and how you can interact with them.

**原文引用：**

> Villages are some of the most bustling, lively places in Minecraft outside of the player’s own constructions. They’re populated by sort-of-friendly folk involved in various useful pursuits: farmers, fisherman, fletchers, butchers, clerics, armorers and more. Including my favourite: the nitwit. Villagers in Minecraft can also be leatherworkers, librarians, masons, cartographers, shepherds, toolsmiths, and weaponsmiths.

> Right click on a villager in Minecraft and you can trade with them, offering them emeralds in exchange for better equipment, maps to notable treasures or food. Unless you are trying to trade with a nitwit, of course, in which case you’re going to get squat. Who’s the nitwit now?

> Back in the old days, though, that’s all you were guaranteed from any of the village’s occupants.

> One of the reasons it took so long to implement trading was that, originally, Minecraft creator Notch wanted trading to take place in the world - you’d drop an item and the villager would pick it up and throw something else back to you. Jens says this was in homage to Dungeon Master, an RPG series that let you trade with vendors by dumping your wares on a table - and the vendor would then look happy or sad. This turned out to be a lot more difficult to implement in the less restricted world of Minecraft. Back to Jens again!

> A fair deal, I’d say, especially now that villager trading has arrived in Pocket/Windows 10 edition! You can see all the other changes in our latest update here.

### Meet the Wandering Trader
- **来源类型**：minecraft.net
- **原文链接**：<https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/meet-wandering-trader>
- **发布时间**：2019-03-22T07:00:00.000+0000
- **页面摘要**：Let us sell you on this Village and Pillage villager!

**原文引用：**

> Imagine you’re out strolling in the Overworld. The sun is shining, the cows are mooing, and the zombies are growling beneath the ground. It’s a beautiful day and everything is as it should be! Then, all of a sudden, you hear a strangely familiar sound - Hrmm!

> A mysterious blue-clad villager has appeared out of nowhere! You gasp and drop your controller. You fall to your knees. Your mind explodes with questions: “How did it get here?”, “Why is it trying to trade with me?” and “Minecraft villagers outside of villages? What blasphemy is this?!”

> Calm down, friend! Everything is fine! You’ve just encountered Minecraft’s latest and most experienced salesman – the Wandering Trader!

> Covered in blue robes and accompanied by two llamas, the mysterious Wandering Trader is just what its name suggests – a travelling salesman, journeying the Overworld in search of customers willing to exchange their shiny emeralds for rare and exotic items.

> Steve Enos is a game designer for Minecraft, and one of several developers that have been involved in the Trader’s design. “The idea for the Wandering Trader came from RPG’s,” he explains. “When the heroes are travelling through an incredibly dangerous place, they usually come across some sort of merchant. It’s obvious that the merchant is there so you can get stocked up on potions or healing items before you fight a boss or something.

> “But we started to think about the merchant's story. Why was it there? Where is it going? We thought it would be neat if the Wandering Trader could travel from village to village, sometimes encountering the player in the wilderness.”

> While trading isn’t exactly a new mechanic in Minecraft, the trader’s mobile behaviour certainly is. They can appear when you least expect it – either out in the overworld or occasionally in villages, appearing at the local gather site. The trader can offer a wide variety of goods, from dyes and flowers to coral blocks or even nautilus shells. Also, since they like to travel, the Wandering Trader has its own way of dealing with hostile mobs.

> “My favorite part is how it drinks a Potion of Invisibility when in danger. It’s funny how you can still see it holding the two llamas by the leads!” says Steve.



### Meet the Nitwit
- **来源类型**：minecraft.net
- **原文链接**：<https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/meet-nitwit>
- **发布时间**：2017-01-23T09:33:59.000+0000
- **页面摘要**：Minecraft’s least useful mob?

**原文引用：**

> The Exploration Update may have bought maps and mansions and other marvels, but arguably it made an even greater contribution to the texture of village living in the shape of the nitwit.

> They may burble benevolently like their fellow villagers, but these green-robed layabouts don’t have any trade at all, and just spend each day pottering about aimlessly. You can wave all the emeralds you want - they have nothing for you. So why are they there?

> So: are they Minecraft’s most useless mob? Only if you assume that everything in Minecraft is there to serve you! Personally, I think the nitwits are just having a jolly good time and/or protesting against the imperatives of the commercial state by denying emerald acquisition through limited trading partners as the ideal system on which to predicate social stability. More power to ‘em. (Also, bats are pretty useless, too.)

### What is an Iron Golem (And How Can You Make One?)
- **来源类型**：minecraft.net
- **原文链接**：<https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/meet-iron-golem>
- **发布时间**：2017-02-26T23:00:00.000+0000
- **页面摘要**：Meet the Iron Golem, a neutral mob in Minecraft that can attack monsters. Learn how to make an Iron Golem that can protect you and your villagers.

**原文引用：**

> Being the wonderful people that we definitely are, we would never attack an innocent villager. Even though they carry great loot that we'd be able to have without paying. Plus, they don't use weapons or wear armour, so they wouldn't be able to defend themselves. In fact, there's a really good chance we'd get away with it. But we'd still never attack a villager because... because... um, actually, why aren't we attacking villagers again?

> We're only joking! Probably! Because there's at least one giant reason not to mess with villagers. Their loyal defenders that can't drown, suffer pain from falling or even be knocked back. Sentient statues that deal huge damage with just a single swing of their mighty arms. We pity you if you're caught attacking villagers – we really do - because then you've just made an enemy of the Iron Golem. Don't be surprised if that's the last thing you ever do - they're one of the hardest hitting mobs in the Overworld.

> Powerful as they are, the Iron golem does have a softer side. Sometimes it'll spawn a poppy in its hand and kindly offer it to a villager. We're green with envy. All an Iron Golem ever offered us was a free arm swing to the face.

> To make an Iron Golem, you'll need four iron blocks and a carved pumpkin. Once you have these materials, place the iron blocks in a "T" shape and put the carved pumpkin on top. This will make an Iron Golem.

> If you don't have the materials to make an Iron Golem, you can find them in any biome. Iron Golems can often be found in villages. Sometimes, Iron Golems can be found at Pillager Outposts. If you find one in a cage and free it, it will help you attack pillagers.

### Iron Golem
- **来源类型**：minecraft.net
- **原文链接**：<https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/iron-golem>
- **发布时间**：2024-09-11T22:00:00.000+0000
- **页面摘要**：In the heat of combat, it can be very easy for friendly fire to occur. A sword is swung, or an arrow fired, and the weapon misses its intended zombie target – hitting a fleeing villager instead. It was an accident, and accidents happen. You’re not a bad person. But you know who won’t forgive and forget? Our mob of the month – the imposing iron golem.

**原文引用：**

> In the heat of combat, it can be very easy for friendly fire to occur. A sword is swung, or an arrow fired, and the weapon misses its intended zombie target – hitting a fleeing villager instead. It was an accident, and accidents happen. You’re not a bad person. But you know who won’t forgive and forget? Our mob of the month – the imposing iron golem.

> These strange constructs first started appearing in villages in March 2012 – back then, updates didn’t have names like they do now. So Mojang’s historians refer to this era in the game’s development by the mysterious name: “Java Edition 1.2.1”. It was around the same time that jungle biomes, bottles of enchanting, redstone lamps and ocelots also appeared.

> Iron golems are naturally found in two places – villages, where they roam around protecting the inhabitants, and pillager outposts, where they’ve been captured in cages. Unlike many mobs, though, they can also be created – place four iron-blocks in a t-shape, and then put a carved pumpkin or jack o’lantern on the top.

> Golems obey a few simple rules. Rule one: wander around until you see danger. Rule two: if you see danger, immediately attack it by flinging it up in the air. Rule three: never attack your creator. That’s more or less it. But this means that you need to be careful around a golem that you didn’t create – if it perceives you as a danger, for example because you harm a villager nearby, then it’ll attack.

> Despite their appearance, iron golems aren’t indestructible. When attacked, their bodies will begin showing cracks. If you want to heal one, use an iron ingot on it to patch it up a bit. If killed, they drop iron ingots and poppy flowers. Why poppies? Well, it turns out that golems also have a soft side – occasionally you might see one offering a poppy to a villager.

> The most famous story about a golem takes place in Prague. In it, a rabbi creates a golem to defend the Jewish population of the city from persecution. Every Friday, before the Sabbath, he’d deactivate the golem by removing its “shem” – a piece of paper with instructions inserted into its head. One Friday, however, he forgot – and the golem became increasingly uncontrollable and violent. Eventually, the rabbi managed to pull the “shem” out of its mouth to immobilize it, and the creature was stored in the attic of the city’s synagogue – where it could one day be restored again to life if needed.

> Today, golems can be found in plenty of fantasy settings – from Terry Pratchett’s Discworld, to Dungeons & Dragons, to our very own Minecraft. And unfortunately, Minecraft’s golems have no shem to pull out if they attack you. Which leaves two options when confronted with an angry iron golem – fight, or flee. Which will you choose?
